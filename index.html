PROLOGUE

The story begins.

Dhaka does not wake so much as it ignites. By 1610 this city on the Buriganga was a Mughal capital; its muslin once dressed emperors, and today some twenty million stories crowd its lanes — every one of them a photograph waiting for the right pair of eyes. This day is built for those eyes.

You will be collected at eight, while the wholesale markets are at their loudest, and returned by late afternoon with memory cards heavy from a city that never once stopped performing. In between lie eight chapters: a modernist railway cathedral from the 1960s, a lane that supplies the city's every celebration, the indigo-scented stacks of a book bazaar, the Mughal-era alleys of the old city and its proudest biriyani, a street of conch-shell artisans, one of the busiest river ports on earth — and, across the water, hulls the size of buildings.

Everything is arranged. All that remains is for you to turn the first page.
